## [tof-patho] Pathophysiology: tetralogy of Fallot

One developmental error, **anterior and cephalad deviation of the outlet septum**, produces all four features:

-   **Malalignment VSD**: large, non-restrictive, under the aorta
-   **RV outflow tract obstruction**: infundibular muscle, a small valve and annulus, sometimes small branch PAs
-   **Overriding aorta**: straddling the defect
-   **RV hypertrophy**: the RV pumps at systemic pressure

Chain: Equal RV and LV pressures (large VSD) → RVOT obstruction sets the shunt → **More obstruction: right-to-left, cyanosis** → Polycythaemia, clubbing, squatting, spells

The spectrum runs from a "pink tet" (mild obstruction, net left-to-right) to severe cyanosis or pulmonary atresia. Untreated, about 55% survive 5 years and 30% 10 years.

<p class="note">The model is an adult heart: in a child the relations are the same and everything is smaller.</p>

> **Evidence:** Merck Manual: four components, shunt direction set by RVOT obstruction; untreated survival 55% at 5 and 30% at 10 years.

## [tof-spell] Hypercyanotic (tet) spell: what to do

Agitation, crying or dehydration → infundibular spasm and falling SVR → more right-to-left shunt → deeper cyanosis, which drives more hyperpnea and acidosis. Break the cycle:

1.  **Calm the child; knee-chest position** (raises SVR and venous return); 100% oxygen
2.  **Morphine** to stop hyperpnea; IV fluid bolus to fill the RV
3.  **Raise SVR: phenylephrine** (1–5 µg/kg IV) or vasopressin
4.  **Beta-blocker** (propranolol, or esmolol infusion) to relax the infundibulum
5.  Sedation, intubation and ventilation if refractory (beware falling SVR on induction); correct acidosis
6.  A spell is an indication for **early surgery**: repair or a shunt

> **Evidence:** Boston Children's CICU: knee-chest, oxygen, morphine, volume, phenylephrine 1–5 µg/kg, beta-blockade; operative repair often indicated after recurrent or even single spells.

## [tof-anatomy] The anatomy the repair must respect

-   The **VSD** lies under the aortic valve; its posteroinferior rim carries the **His bundle** (perimembranous extension), as in an isolated perimembranous VSD
-   The **infundibulum**: hypertrophied septal and parietal bands; resect enough, but not the moderator band or the septal attachments of the tricuspid valve
-   The **pulmonary valve and annulus**: measured with Hegar dilators against normal values (z-score)
-   The **coronaries**: look before any ventriculotomy; a major coronary (for example the LAD arising from the right coronary) crossing the RV outflow changes the plan (conduit or limited incision)

<p class="note">The model is an adult heart: in a child the relations are the same and everything is smaller.</p>

## [tof-case] Case: a 3-year-old with tetralogy

Late presentation is the rule here. Complete repair is still the aim; the question is whether the pulmonary arteries and annulus allow it.

> **Evidence:** Schaffner et al. 2022: 165 humanitarian patients (many from sub-Saharan Africa), median age 4.5 years at repair: no early deaths in 161 complete repairs; transannular patch in 38%; preserved valve function linked to shorter ventilation and ICU stay.

### Case

A **3-year-old girl, 11 kg**, from Kisii, referred with cyanosis since infancy, squatting after play and two spells this year. SpO₂ 74%, clubbing, single S2, harsh ejection murmur. Hb 19 g/dL. Echo: tetralogy with a large malalignment VSD, infundibular and valvar stenosis, **pulmonary annulus 9 mm (z-score about −2)**, confluent branch PAs of good size, left arch, no major coronary across the RVOT.

## [tof-decision] Repair, shunt first, and keeping the valve

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| When? | Elective complete repair usually at about 2–6 months (earlier for spells or severe cyanosis); a late presenter is repaired when seen |
| Shunt first? | Small or sick neonates, very small PAs, complex anatomy or a coronary across the RVOT: modified BT shunt (or RVOT/duct stent), repair later |
| Keep the valve? | Preserve the annulus when it can be opened enough; a transannular patch relieves obstruction at the cost of free pulmonary regurgitation and later RV dilatation |
| Which z-score? | No single cut-off: z-scores depend on the normal dataset used. Decide on the annulus achieved and the residual gradient after valvotomy |
| Success off bypass | Low residual RV pressure (RV:LV ratio well under systemic; many accept up to about 0.7) and no significant residual VSD on TOE |

> **Evidence:** Merck: elective repair at 2–6 months; shunt or RVOT stent for low birth weight or complex anatomy. Awori et al. (EJCTS 2013; WJPCHS 2018, from Nairobi): the same z-score means very different annulus sizes across normal datasets, so a rigid z-score cut-off for a transannular patch is unreliable.

## [tof-repair-consent] Consent: what to discuss with this patient

**1\. The patient's own risk**: Age and weight, cyanosis and hematocrit, PA size, coronary anatomy. Quote the figure, not a textbook average.

#### 2\. Common

-   ICU for a few days
-   Drains, pacing wires
-   Some desaturation at first if a PFO is left open

#### 3\. Serious

-   Low cardiac output from a stiff RV ("restrictive physiology")
-   Junctional ectopic tachycardia
-   Complete heart block
-   Residual VSD or RVOT obstruction needing re-operation
-   Death (under 5% for uncomplicated repair)

#### 4\. Specific to this operation

-   Pulmonary regurgitation, especially after a transannular patch: lifelong follow-up and likely pulmonary valve replacement later
-   Late arrhythmias

#### 5\. Alternatives

-   BT shunt or RVOT stent first, repair later
-   No surgery: progressive cyanosis, spells, stroke, brain abscess, early death
-   No operation, and what that means

#### 6\. Recovery

-   ICU, then ward; drains; walking from day 1
-   Return to normal activity over weeks
-   Follow-up in clinic

#### 7\. Kenya-specific

-   Waiting time; spells while waiting (propranolol)
-   Follow-up echo access for decades

## [tof-sternotomy] Median sternotomy

Median sternotomy; in a small child keep a **thymic remnant** (or excise part of it) and harvest a piece of **anterior pericardium** for the patch before heparin. Fix it in 0.6% glutaraldehyde for a few minutes if you want it stiffer and easier to handle.

## [tof-cannulate] Cannulation: aorta and both cavae

Heparin; **aortic** cannula high on the ascending aorta, **bicaval** venous drainage (SVC directly or through the appendage, IVC low on the right atrium), with **snares** around both cavae so the right atrium can be opened without air entering the venous line.

Bicaval cannulation is the rule for any operation inside the right atrium, as here for tetralogy.

## [tof-clamp] Cross-clamp and cardioplegia

Mild hypothermia, cross-clamp and **antegrade cold cardioplegia** into the aortic root; tighten the caval snares. A **left heart vent** (through the right superior pulmonary vein or across the defect) keeps the field dry from bronchial return.

> **Evidence:** AATS/TSRA primer: median sternotomy, aortic and bicaval cannulation, cold cardioplegia after cross-clamping; for VSD an LA or LV vent through the right superior pulmonary vein and repeat doses every 20–30 minutes.

## [tof-ra] Right atriotomy

Open the right atrium **parallel to the atrioventricular groove**, about 1 cm from it, from the appendage toward the IVC cannula; stay away from the **crista terminalis and the SA node** at the SVC–atrial junction. Retract the edges with stay sutures.

## [tof-vsd] Close the VSD through the right atrium

Through the tricuspid valve (retract or detach the septal leaflet) close the VSD with a **Dacron or pericardial patch**, baffling the LV to the aorta. Shallow bites on the RV side at the **posteroinferior rim** (His bundle); along the superior rim, sutures pass close to the aortic valve. The muscular rim toward the outlet is sewn through the hypertrophied septal band.

## [tof-rvot] Open the RV outflow: resect, valvotomy, measure

Through the right atrium and tricuspid valve, or a limited infundibular incision: **divide and resect the obstructing septal and parietal bands**. Through the pulmonary trunk: **commissurotomy** of the fused valve. Then pass **Hegar dilators** across the annulus and compare with the expected size for the child's body surface area.

## [tof-tap] Transannular patch, if the annulus is too small

If the annulus remains too small: **extend the incision across the annulus** onto the pulmonary trunk and close it with a **pericardial transannular patch**, keeping the RV incision as short as possible. A monocusp can reduce early regurgitation.

Off bypass: measure **RV and LV pressures** and check for a residual VSD on TOE. A high RV:LV ratio means residual obstruction to relieve before leaving theatre.

### Question

**Q:** What is the long-term price of a transannular patch?
- [x] Free pulmonary regurgitation, leading to RV dilatation and later pulmonary valve replacement
  > Opening the annulus removes the valve function; the RV dilates over years.
- [ ] Recurrent VSD
- [ ] Aortic stenosis
- [ ] Complete heart block

## [tof-close] De-air, close the atrium, release the clamp

Before the last sutures: **fill the left heart** (anesthetist inflates the lungs), let air escape through the defect or the patch edge, then tie. Close the right atriotomy in two layers of running polypropylene, release the snares, **vent the aortic root** and remove the cross-clamp.

Leave a small **patent foramen ovale** in a hypertrophied, stiff RV: it lets the right heart off-load early (at the cost of some desaturation).

## [tof-decannulate] Separate from bypass; check the repair

Rewarm, separate from bypass, **transesophageal echo** for a residual shunt and valve function, then decannulate and give protamine. Temporary **atrial and ventricular pacing wires**; one or two drains; close the pericardium loosely or leave it open.

## [tof-repair-icu] ICU and post-operative care

Start with the [cardiac core](#approach=cticu-cardiac&step=0), the [lab schedule](#approach=cticu-core&step=0) and the [escalation table](#approach=cticu-core&step=2); then this operation's own points.

#### Specific to this operation

-   Restrictive RV: low output with high CVP and a small, stiff RV; keep preload, avoid high airway pressures, milrinone; early extubation helps
-   JET: cool to about 35 °C, correct Mg²⁺ and K⁺, reduce catecholamines, amiodarone; atrial pacing faster than the JET
-   Echo: residual VSD, RVOT gradient, pulmonary regurgitation, effusions; chylothorax
-   Children: doses by weight with the pediatric intensivist

#### Labs

-   Day 1 morning: FBC, UEC, LFTs, Ca, Mg, PO₄ <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>

Doses: [electrolytes](#approach=cticu-doses&step=0), [vasoactive drugs](#approach=cticu-doses&step=1).

> **Evidence:** Merck: perioperative mortality under 5% for uncomplicated repair.
