## [tv2-patho] Pathophysiology: thoracic aortic aneurysm

**Medial degeneration** (loss of smooth muscle cells, fragmented elastic fibers, pooled proteoglycans) weakens the wall. Causes: **heritable** (Marfan syndrome, Loeys-Dietz, familial, bicuspid aortic valve), **degenerative/atherosclerotic** (the descending aorta in older smokers with hypertension), and **inflammatory or infective**: **syphilitic aortitis** (ascending and arch; obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum) and **Takayasu arteritis** (young women), both seen in Africa.

Chain: Medial degeneration → Dilatation → Laplace: tension rises with radius → **Dissection or rupture**

**Hinge points**: the risk of rupture or dissection jumps at about **6.0 cm in the ascending** and **7.0 cm in the descending** aorta, so repair is advised before them: **ascending 5.5 cm** (5.0 cm in Marfan, or with risk features, or in experienced teams), **descending 5.5 cm** for TEVAR with suitable anatomy (ESC 2024; ACC/AHA 2022 uses 6.0 cm for descending and thoracoabdominal), **thoracoabdominal 6.0 cm**, or growth of 0.5 cm in a year (0.3 cm a year for 2 years).

**Crawford extent** of thoracoabdominal aneurysm: I (left subclavian to above the renals), II (left subclavian to the bifurcation: the largest, highest spinal risk), III (distal thoracic to the bifurcation), IV (below the diaphragm), V (distal thoracic to above the renals, Safi).

**The spinal cord** depends on the anterior spinal artery, fed at the lower thoracic level mainly by the **artery of Adamkiewicz** (usually from a left intercostal artery at T9–T12). Covering or clamping that segment risks **paraplegia**: protect the cord with perfusion pressure (MAP), CSF drainage and, in open repair, distal perfusion and intercostal reattachment.

> **Evidence:** natural history: Coady et al. (J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 1997): hinge points 6.0 cm ascending, 7.0 cm descending; median size at rupture or dissection 6.0 and 7.2 cm. Thresholds: 2022 ACC/AHA guideline (Isselbacher et al.) and 2024 EACTS/STS guideline (Czerny et al.). Crawford/Safi extents: Frederick and Woo, Ann Cardiothorac Surg 2012. Syphilitic aortitis: ascending 50%, arch 35%, descending 15% (ICVTS 2012).

**Normal sizes and when to act.** An aneurysm is a permanent dilatation to at least **1.5 times** the expected normal diameter.

| Artery | Normal adult (typical) | Aneurysmal | Usual threshold to repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ascending aorta | about 2.2–3.6 cm | dilated above 22 mm/m² (ESC) | 5.5 cm; 5.0 cm with risk factors, bicuspid root phenotype or Marfan; 4.5 cm if the aortic valve is being operated on |
| Descending thoracic | about 2.0–3.0 cm | dilated above 16 mm/m² (ESC) | TEVAR at 5.5 cm with suitable anatomy (ESC 2024); 6.0 cm (ACC/AHA 2022) |
| Infrarenal aorta | about 1.5 cm (women), 1.7 cm (men) over 50 | 3.0 cm or more | 5.5 cm (men), 5.0 cm (women); growth of about 1 cm a year; symptoms |
| Common iliac | about 1 cm | about 1.8–2.0 cm and more (definitions vary) | 4.0 cm (ESVS 2024, raised from 3.5 cm) |
| Popliteal | about 0.5–0.9 cm | over 1.5 cm or 1.5 × normal | 2.0 cm, or smaller with thrombus and embolism or poor run-off (SVS 2022) |

> **Evidence:** definition of aneurysm as 1.5 × normal: SVS/ISCVS reporting standards (Johnston et al., J Vasc Surg 1991); normal infrarenal diameters (AAFP 2006). Thoracic indexing and thresholds: ESC 2024 (Mazzolai et al.) and ACC/AHA 2022 (Isselbacher et al.). Iliac threshold: ESVS 2024 (Recommendation 135). Popliteal: SVS 2022 (Farber et al.). Normal ranges for the iliac and popliteal arteries are typical values, not guideline cut-offs.

## [tv2-anatomy] The descending thoracic aorta and what lies around it

From the left subclavian artery (zone 3) down to the hiatus at T12. On its left: the **vagus** and, at the arch, the **left recurrent laryngeal nerve** hooking round the ligamentum arteriosum; medially the **esophagus** (and the thoracic duct behind); the **intercostal arteries** leave its back in pairs; the **hemiazygos** veins cross behind. The **artery of Adamkiewicz** usually arises between T9 and T12 on the left.

Aortic zones for TEVAR (Ishimaru): 0 ascending to the innominate; 1 to the left carotid; 2 to the left subclavian; 3 the proximal descending; 4 the rest of the descending.

## [tv2-case] Case: a degenerative descending aneurysm in an older man

**TEVAR**: a 6.4 cm degenerative aneurysm in a 71-year-old with COPD, with **landing zones of at least 20 mm** of healthy aorta at both ends and iliac access that takes the sheath. The proximal landing needs coverage of the **left subclavian artery** (zone 2): **revascularize it first** (carotid-subclavian bypass or transposition), which lowers stroke, arm ischemia and spinal cord risk.

> **Evidence:** VALOR (J Vasc Surg 2008): 30-day mortality 2.1% with TEVAR vs 7.9% with open repair (historical controls), paraplegia 1.5% and stroke 3.6% after TEVAR. Cheng et al. (JACC 2010, 5,888 patients): TEVAR lower 30-day mortality (OR 0.44) and paraplegia (OR 0.42), no long-term survival difference. SVS 2009 (Matsumura et al.): routine revascularization before elective TEVAR that covers the left subclavian (weak recommendation); strongly recommended with a LIMA graft or dominant left vertebral. Spinal cord injury meta-analysis (Ann Cardiothorac Surg 2023; 61,962 patients): descending aneurysms 2.0% after TEVAR and after open repair.

### Case

A **71-year-old man**, smoker, COPD (FEV1 48%), hypertension. CT: a **6.4 cm** fusiform aneurysm of the mid-descending aorta, starting **12 mm** beyond the left subclavian origin, ending 4 cm above the coeliac trunk. External iliac arteries 8 mm. A dominant left vertebral artery.

## [tv2-deploy] Access, angiography, deploy, completion

A CSF drain if the coverage is long (over about 20 cm), the previous abdominal aorta has been repaired, or the subclavian and hypogastric supply is compromised. Femoral access (percutaneous with preclosure, or a cut-down; an iliac conduit if the vessels are small). A pigtail from the other groin or the left arm; an angiogram in the left anterior oblique projection to open the arch.

Advance the stent graft over a stiff wire, place its covered edge at the planned zone, lower the blood pressure (systolic about 90–100 mmHg or rapid pacing) and **deploy**; balloon the seal zones only if needed. Completion angiogram: no type I endoleak, the carotids patent.

## [tv2-after] Neurology checks; surveillance

Leg and arm checks, MAP above 80–90 mmHg for the first days, drain management. **Surveillance** imaging at about 1 month, then yearly: endoleak, migration, sac growth, retrograde dissection.
