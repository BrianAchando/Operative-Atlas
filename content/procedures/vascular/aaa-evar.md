## [ae-patho] Pathophysiology: abdominal aortic aneurysm

**What fails is the media.** Elastin is broken down (matrix metalloproteinases from macrophages and smooth muscle cells), smooth muscle cells die, and chronic inflammation involves the adventitia. The infrarenal aorta has the fewest vasa vasorum and the least elastin: most aneurysms start there. Risk factors: **age, male sex, smoking** (the strongest modifiable), family history, hypertension; diabetes is, oddly, protective.

Chain: Elastin degradation, loss of smooth muscle → Dilatation → Wall tension rises with radius (Laplace: T = P × r) → **More dilatation, faster growth** → **Rupture**

An aneurysm is a diameter of **3.0 cm or more**. Growth accelerates as it enlarges; rupture risk climbs steeply above 5.5 cm:

| Diameter | 1-year risk of (probable) rupture, untreated |
| --- | --- |
| 5.5–5.9 cm | 9.4% |
| 6.0–6.9 cm | 10.2% (19.1% at 6.5–6.9) |
| 7.0 cm or more | 32.5% |

**Repair thresholds**: 5.5 cm in men, 5.0 cm in women (smaller aortas rupture at smaller diameters), rapid growth (about 1 cm a year, confirmed), or symptoms (tenderness, back pain). Below that, **surveillance**: early repair of 4.0–5.5 cm aneurysms did not improve survival.

**The neck** decides the operation:

| Type (SVS 2021) | Where the aneurysm starts | Proximal clamp and repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Infrarenal** | a healthy neck of 10–15 mm or more below the lowest renal artery | infrarenal clamp; EVAR usually possible |
| Short neck | neck 4–10 mm | often suprarenal clamp; EVAR marginal |
| **Juxtarenal** | at the renal arteries (neck 4 mm or less), not involving them | suprarenal clamp, anastomosis at the renal origins; or fenestrated EVAR |
| **Pararenal / paravisceral** ("suprarenal") | involving the renal arteries, up to (pararenal) or including (paravisceral) the SMA | supracoeliac clamp, renal and visceral reconstruction; or fenestrated/branched EVAR |
| Thoracoabdominal extent IV | up to the coeliac axis or diaphragmatic hiatus | thoracoabdominal repair |

**In Africa** aortic aneurysms present younger and more often from **infection or HIV-associated vasculitis** (saccular, at atypical sites, sometimes multiple); many present ruptured. Always consider an infective (mycotic) aneurysm: fever, raised inflammatory markers, a saccular or lobulated shape, periaortic gas or fluid.

> **Evidence:** rupture rates: Lederle et al., JAMA 2002 (198 patients refusing or unfit for repair). Small aneurysms: UK Small Aneurysm Trial 12-year follow-up (Powell et al., Br J Surg 2007: no survival difference, HR 0.90) and ADAM (NEJM 2002: RR 1.21). Thresholds and surveillance: ESVS 2024 (Wanhainen et al.). Anatomical definitions: SVS reporting standards (Oderich et al., J Vasc Surg 2021). HIV-associated aneurysms: Nair et al. (J Vasc Surg 1999; mean age 30, often multiple, atypical sites). African AAA: Wu, J West Afr Coll Surg 2022.

**Normal sizes and when to act.** An aneurysm is a permanent dilatation to at least **1.5 times** the expected normal diameter.

| Artery | Normal adult (typical) | Aneurysmal | Usual threshold to repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ascending aorta | about 2.2–3.6 cm | dilated above 22 mm/m² (ESC) | 5.5 cm; 5.0 cm with risk factors, bicuspid root phenotype or Marfan; 4.5 cm if the aortic valve is being operated on |
| Descending thoracic | about 2.0–3.0 cm | dilated above 16 mm/m² (ESC) | TEVAR at 5.5 cm with suitable anatomy (ESC 2024); 6.0 cm (ACC/AHA 2022) |
| Infrarenal aorta | about 1.5 cm (women), 1.7 cm (men) over 50 | 3.0 cm or more | 5.5 cm (men), 5.0 cm (women); growth of about 1 cm a year; symptoms |
| Common iliac | about 1 cm | about 1.8–2.0 cm and more (definitions vary) | 4.0 cm (ESVS 2024, raised from 3.5 cm) |
| Popliteal | about 0.5–0.9 cm | over 1.5 cm or 1.5 × normal | 2.0 cm, or smaller with thrombus and embolism or poor run-off (SVS 2022) |

> **Evidence:** definition of aneurysm as 1.5 × normal: SVS/ISCVS reporting standards (Johnston et al., J Vasc Surg 1991); normal infrarenal diameters (AAFP 2006). Thoracic indexing and thresholds: ESC 2024 (Mazzolai et al.) and ACC/AHA 2022 (Isselbacher et al.). Iliac threshold: ESVS 2024 (Recommendation 135). Popliteal: SVS 2022 (Farber et al.). Normal ranges for the iliac and popliteal arteries are typical values, not guideline cut-offs.

### Question

**Q:** Why does an aneurysm grow faster as it gets bigger?
- [x] Wall tension rises with the radius (Laplace's law), so the larger the aneurysm, the greater the stress on an already weakened wall
  > T = P × r: at the same blood pressure a 6 cm aneurysm carries twice the wall tension of a 3 cm aorta. That is why growth and rupture risk accelerate with size.
- [ ] Blood flow slows
- [ ] Thrombus lining the sac weakens it
- [ ] Blood pressure rises with size

## [ae-anatomy] The infrarenal aorta and its neighbors

From above down on the front of the aorta: the **coeliac trunk** (T12/L1), the **SMA** about 1 cm lower, the **renal arteries** (L1/L2, the right passing behind the IVC), the **left renal vein** crossing in front of the aorta just below the SMA (the upper limit of the infrarenal neck), the **IMA** from the left front at L3, and the bifurcation at L4.

What to protect: the **left renal vein** above the neck (and a retroaortic or circumaortic variant, which tears when clamping); the **duodenum** (3rd and 4th parts) over the aneurysm; the **IVC** to the right and the **iliac veins** behind the iliac arteries (the left common iliac vein runs behind the right common iliac artery): encircling the iliac arteries tears them; the **ureters** crossing the iliac bifurcations; the **superior hypogastric plexus** on the left of the aorta and over the left common iliac (dissect on the right to preserve ejaculation); the **lumbar arteries** behind, which back-bleed into the opened sac.

> **Note:** the reference CT ends at the 4th lumbar vertebra; the iliac and femoral vessels and the groins are drawn to typical adult dimensions below it.

### Question

**Q:** Which vein lies behind the right common iliac artery and is torn by careless encircling of that artery?
- [x] The left common iliac vein (near the confluence of the IVC)
  > The iliac veins lie behind and slightly to the right of the arteries; the left common iliac vein crosses behind the right common iliac artery to reach the IVC. Clamp the iliacs without encircling them.
- [ ] The left renal vein
- [ ] The inferior mesenteric vein
- [ ] The gonadal vein

## [ae-case] Case: EVAR in a man with COPD

**Anatomy suits EVAR**: a neck of at least 10–15 mm, not too angled or conical, of a diameter the device can seal (usually 18–32 mm), and iliac access vessels wide enough for the sheath (about 7 mm or more for most devices). **His lungs** make a laparotomy and a long aortic clamp riskier: EVAR avoids both.

**The contract**: lifelong surveillance (CT or duplex) for endoleaks and migration, and a greater chance of a secondary procedure.

> **Evidence:** EVAR-1 (Lancet 2016): lower early mortality with EVAR, lost over time; more reinterventions. ESVS 2024: EVAR is the preferred modality in most patients with suitable anatomy; for a ruptured AAA, EVAR is the first option when anatomy allows (Class I). IMPROVE (BMJ 2014 and 2017): 30-day mortality 35.4% vs 37.4% for an endovascular strategy vs open repair in rupture, with better 3-year survival (48% vs 56% mortality) and quality of life.

### Case

A **77-year-old man**, COPD (FEV1 45%), ischemic heart disease with a stent 3 years ago. A **5.9 cm** infrarenal aneurysm; neck **24 mm long**, 23 mm wide, angle 35°; common iliacs 13 mm and not aneurysmal; external iliacs 8 mm. Lives in Nairobi near a hospital with CT.

### Question

**Q:** Which anatomical feature is essential for a standard EVAR?
- [x] A healthy infrarenal neck long enough for a seal (usually at least 10–15 mm), not too angled or conical
  > The stent graft seals by radial force against normal aorta below the renal arteries; a short, wide, angled or thrombus-lined neck causes type I endoleak and failure.
- [ ] A small aneurysm sac
- [ ] A patent IMA
- [ ] Calcified iliac arteries

## [ae-access] Femoral access; mark the renal arteries

Ultrasound-guided puncture of both **common femoral arteries** (over the femoral head), **preclosure** sutures placed (or a surgical cut-down in calcified or small arteries). Heparin. A stiff wire to the thoracic aorta; an angiogram with a marker catheter, the image angled to open the neck, to **mark the lowest renal artery**.

> **Note:** the reference CT ends at the 4th lumbar vertebra; the iliac and femoral vessels and the groins are drawn to typical adult dimensions below it.

## [ae-deploy] Deploy the main body below the lowest renal; cannulate the gate; the limbs

Introduce the **main body** through one groin, align its top marker just below the lowest renal artery, and deploy (suprarenal fixation, where the device has it, sits across the renal arteries without covering them). **Cannulate the contralateral gate** from the other groin, confirm you are inside the graft, and deploy the **contralateral limb**, then the ipsilateral limb, landing both in the common iliac arteries above the internal iliac origins. Balloon the seal zones and junctions.

### Question

**Q:** The completion angiogram shows contrast filling the sac from around the top of the graft. What is it, and what now?
- [x] A type I (proximal seal) endoleak: treat it now (balloon, proximal cuff or anchors)
  > Type I and III endoleaks pressurize the sac and must be fixed; a type II (from lumbars or IMA) is usually observed.
- [ ] A type II endoleak: observe
- [ ] Normal: it will thrombose
- [ ] Convert to open repair immediately

## [ae-surv] Completion angiogram, closure, lifelong surveillance

Completion angiogram: both renal arteries patent, no **type I** (seal) or **type III** (junction) endoleak, both internal iliacs perfused. Close the arteries. **Surveillance**: CT angiography (or contrast ultrasound) at about 30 days, then by protocol, lifelong: sac growth means an endoleak until proven otherwise.

| Endoleak | Source | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | seal zone (proximal a, distal b) | treat |
| II | lumbar arteries or IMA back-filling | observe; treat if the sac grows |
| III | junction or fabric tear | treat |
| IV | graft porosity | resolves |
| V | endotension (growth without visible leak) | investigate |
