## [ciaa-patho] Iliac artery aneurysm

Most iliac aneurysms are **common iliac**, usually **with an AAA** (up to 40% of AAA patients have one); isolated iliac aneurysms are uncommon. The internal iliac is the next commonest; the external iliac is rarely aneurysmal. Same disease as AAA: degeneration of the media, smoking, hypertension, age, male sex. Deep in the pelvis they are **hard to feel** and often present large, or ruptured.

| Diameter (common iliac) | Meaning (ESVS 2024) |
| --- | --- |
| About 1 cm | Normal |
| 20–24 mm | Aneurysmal: ultrasound every 3 years |
| 25–29 mm | Every 2 years |
| 30 mm or more | Every year |
| **40 mm** | **Consider elective repair** (threshold raised from 35 mm) |

-   Pressure effects: ureteric obstruction (hydronephrosis), iliac vein compression (leg swelling, DVT), rarely rupture into the iliac vein (arteriovenous fistula)
-   Isolated common iliac aneurysms grow slowly (about 1 mm a year or less in most); a few grow fast

> **Evidence:** ESVS 2024 Rec 134 (IIa): surveillance every 3 years for 20–24 mm, 2 years for 25–29 mm, yearly from 30 mm; Rec 135 (IIa): elective repair at 40 mm (raised from 35 mm in 2019). Steenberge et al. (J Vasc Surg 2022): growth of isolated CIA aneurysms. Bresler et al. 2024: CIA aneurysm in up to 40% of AAA.

## [ciaa-anat] The iliac bifurcation and what lies on it

The common iliac runs from the aortic bifurcation (L4) to the pelvic brim in front of the sacroiliac joint, where it divides into the **external iliac** (to the groin) and the **internal iliac** (to the pelvis: buttock, bowel, bladder, erectile tissue).

-   **The ureter crosses the iliac bifurcation**: it can be stuck to an aneurysm, and must be seen and protected
-   The **iliac veins** lie behind; the left common iliac vein crosses behind the right common iliac artery: a tear here bleeds torrentially
-   The **internal iliac** supplies the gluteal muscles, the rectum and the pelvic organs: keep at least one patent
-   A **landing zone** for a stent graft needs a healthy segment: 10–15 mm of normal external (and internal) iliac

> **Evidence:** Preserving at least one internal iliac artery is recommended (ESVS, as cited by Bresler et al. 2024).

## [ciaa-case] A 4.2 cm right common iliac aneurysm

A **69-year-old man**, smoker, had a CT for hematuria: a **4.2 cm right common iliac aneurysm**, a normal-calibre aorta (2.2 cm), a normal external iliac and a healthy right internal iliac of 8 mm. Mild right hydronephrosis. He walks 2 km a day and is sexually active.

### Question

**Q:** Which repair best preserves his pelvic circulation?
- [x] EVAR with an iliac branch device into the right internal iliac
  > He is at the 40 mm threshold, active and sexually active: an iliac branch device keeps antegrade flow to the internal iliac and avoids buttock claudication and erectile dysfunction from coiling it.
- [ ] Coil the internal iliac and extend the limb into the external iliac
- [ ] Surveillance in 3 years
- [ ] Ligate the common iliac

## [ciaa-decide] Which repair?

| Option | Suits | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **EVAR with an iliac branch device** | Suitable anatomy (landing zones in the external and internal iliac, not too tortuous); preferred to keep pelvic flow | Cost, device availability, branch occlusion |
| EVAR with **internal iliac coil embolization** and an extension into the external iliac | Anatomy unsuitable for a branch; the other internal iliac is patent | **Buttock claudication about 28–31%; new erectile dysfunction about 17%**; rarely colonic or spinal ischemia, especially if both sides are lost |
| **Open repair** (interposition graft, internal iliac re-implanted) | Young and fit, unsuitable anatomy, infection, no access to devices | A big operation; ureter and iliac vein at risk |

-   Choose by patient and lesion (ESVS Rec 136)
-   **Never sacrifice both internal iliacs** if it can be avoided
-   Isolated internal iliac aneurysm: coil the outflow branches and the aneurysm, or a branch device

> **Evidence:** ESVS 2024 Rec 136 (IIa): technique by patient and lesion characteristics; preserve at least one internal iliac (ESVS, cited by Bresler 2024). Rayt et al. 2008 (pooled 634 patients): buttock claudication 28% (unilateral 31%, bilateral 35%), new erectile dysfunction 17% after internal iliac embolization.

## [ciaa-consent] Consent: what to discuss with this patient

**1\. The patient's own risk**: Fitness for open repair, renal function (contrast), the anatomy on CTA. Quote the figure, not a textbook average.

#### 2\. Common

-   Groin wound problems (EVAR)
-   Lifelong scans after a stent graft

#### 3\. Serious

-   Endoleak and further procedures
-   Branch occlusion (internal iliac)
-   Buttock claudication, erectile dysfunction (if the internal iliac is blocked)
-   Bowel ischemia
-   Ureteric injury (open)
-   Death

#### 4\. Specific to this operation

-   With a branch device: a second puncture (arm or the other groin)
-   With coiling: possible pain in the buttock on walking, which often improves

#### 5\. Alternatives

-   Open repair
-   Surveillance below 40 mm
-   No operation, and what that means

#### 6\. Recovery

-   Home in 1–3 days after EVAR; 1–2 weeks after open repair
-   CTA at 30 days, then surveillance

#### 7\. Kenya-specific

-   Branch devices may need importing: cost and waiting time
-   Who will do and pay for lifelong CT surveillance

## [ciaa-ibd] EVAR with an iliac branch device

Bilateral femoral access (and often a brachial or contralateral up-and-over route). The **iliac branch device** is deployed first, landing in the **external iliac**; through its side gate the **internal iliac is cannulated and bridged with a covered stent**. Then the bifurcated aortic body, with its limb docking into the branch device; the contralateral limb as usual. Completion angiogram: no endoleak, both iliac outflows open.

## [ciaa-coil] Alternative: coil the internal iliac, extend into the external iliac

When a branch device is not possible: **coil the main internal iliac trunk** (proximal to its division, to keep the collaterals between the gluteal branches) a few days before or at the EVAR, then extend the iliac limb into the **external iliac**, sealing below the aneurysm. The coils stop back-bleeding into the sac (a type II endoleak).

-   Coil proximally, not into the gluteal branches
-   Stage bilateral embolization if both sides must be lost, and preserve flow on one side wherever possible

## [ciaa-open] Open repair: interposition graft

Retroperitoneal (flank) or midline transperitoneal approach. Identify and sling the **ureter** first. Control the distal aorta or contralateral common iliac, the external iliac and the internal iliac. Open the sac, oversew back-bleeding branches from inside, and sew in a 10–12 mm Dacron **interposition graft** from the common iliac origin to the iliac bifurcation or external iliac, **re-implanting the internal iliac** (or a side limb to it). Close the sac over the graft.

## [ciaa-icu] ICU and post-operative care

Start with the [vascular core](#approach=cticu-vascular&step=0), the [lab schedule](#approach=cticu-core&step=0) and the [escalation table](#approach=cticu-core&step=2); then this operation's own points.

#### Specific to this operation

-   Leg pulses and groin wounds hourly for 6 hours (EVAR access)
-   Buttock pain on walking: internal iliac occlusion (expected after coiling; a new symptom after a branch device suggests branch occlusion: duplex or CT)
-   **Bowel**: bloody diarrhea or rising lactate after internal iliac loss: sigmoidoscopy for colonic ischemia
-   Urine output and creatinine (contrast); the ureter after open repair (flank pain, rising creatinine, urine in the drain)
-   CTA at 30 days, then surveillance as for EVAR; antiplatelet and statin lifelong

#### Labs

-   Day 1: FBC, UEC, lactate if bowel concern

Doses: [electrolytes](#approach=cticu-doses&step=0), [vasoactive drugs](#approach=cticu-doses&step=1).

> **Evidence:** ESVS 2024: early (30-day) CTA after EVAR; iliac diameter over 20 mm is a high-risk feature for annual imaging (Endovascular Today 2024).

### Question

**Q:** Two days after EVAR with bilateral internal iliac coiling, a patient has bloody diarrhea and a lactate of 4. Most likely?
- [x] Ischemic colitis from loss of pelvic flow
  > Bilateral internal iliac loss removes the collateral supply to the sigmoid and rectum; ischemic colitis needs urgent endoscopy and may need a laparotomy.
- [ ] Antibiotic-associated colitis from cefazolin
- [ ] Acute diverticulitis of the sigmoid colon
- [ ] A delayed reaction to the iodinated contrast
