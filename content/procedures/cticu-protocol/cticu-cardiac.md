## [cticu-cardiac-0] Arrival, hemodynamics, bleeding, ventilation

#### Arrival (first 30 min)

-   Structured handover: surgeon, anesthetist, nurse: the operation, bypass and clamp times, how the heart came off bypass, drugs running, pacing, drains
-   ABG, Hb, K⁺, glucose, lactate, ACT or coagulation screen, chest X-ray, ECG; test the epicardial pacing wires

#### Hemodynamics

-   MAP 65–80 mmHg; higher after CABG with carotid disease, lower with a friable aortic suture line
-   Noradrenaline for vasodilatation, adding adrenaline for low output: the anesthetist's choice <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span> ([doses](#approach=cticu-doses&step=1))
-   Low output: preload, rhythm and pacing first; then inotrope; then IABP. **Never miss tamponade**

#### Bleeding

-   Re-explore for more than 400 mL in the first hour, more than 300 mL/h for 2–3 h, more than 200 mL/h for 4 h, a sudden gush, or tamponade physiology
-   Correct temperature, protamine (by ACT), platelets, fibrinogen, calcium first; do not strip the drains
-   Transfuse restrictively: Hb under 7.5 g/dL (TRICS III) <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>

#### Ventilation

-   Extubate within 6 h when warm, awake, not bleeding, stable, with good gases <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>

> **Evidence:** ERAS Cardiac Society guidelines (Engelman et al., JAMA Surg 2019): early extubation, glucose, multimodal analgesia, AKI prevention. TRICS III (Mazer et al., NEJM 2017): restrictive threshold 7.5 g/dL non-inferior.

### Question

**Q:** When is re-exploration indicated for bleeding?
- [x] More than 400 mL in the first hour, or sustained high output, or tamponade
  > Correct coagulopathy and temperature, but these thresholds call for the surgeon.
- [ ] Any drainage at all
- [ ] Only when Hb is under 5 g/dL, as drain volume alone is too unreliable to act on
- [ ] Never in the first 24 h, because coagulopathy must be fully corrected before reopening

## [cticu-cardiac-1] Rhythm, kidneys, glucose, analgesia, day 1

#### Rhythm

-   K⁺ 4.0–5.0 mmol/L; keep Mg²⁺ replete ([doses](#approach=cticu-doses&step=0))
-   AF in 20–40%, peaking on days 2–3: beta-blocker prophylaxis, rate or rhythm control; anticoagulate if it lasts beyond 48 h, once bleeding allows

#### Kidneys, glucose, analgesia, delirium

-   AKI prevention: perfusion pressure, no nephrotoxins, urine output (KDIGO)
-   Glucose under 10 mmol/L (180 mg/dL) <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>
-   Paracetamol-based multimodal analgesia; avoid NSAIDs
-   Screen for delirium (CAM-ICU); mobilize early

#### Day 1

-   Day-1 labs: FBC, UEC, LFTs, Ca, Mg, PO₄ <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>
-   Drains out when output is low and serous
-   Sit out, walk, physiotherapy

> **Evidence:** KDIGO AKI guideline 2012; ERAS Cardiac 2019 (glucose under 180 mg/dL, AF prophylaxis).

### Question

**Q:** Target K⁺ after cardiac surgery?
- [x] 4.0–5.0 mmol/L
  > Low K⁺ and Mg²⁺ provoke AF and ventricular arrhythmias.
- [ ] 3.0–3.5 mmol/L
- [ ] 5.5–6.0 mmol/L
- [ ] Any value

## [cticu-cardiac-2] Anticoagulation after valve surgery

#### Mechanical valve

-   Day 1 morning, or once bleeding has stopped: enoxaparin (Clexane), bridging to warfarin <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>
-   First INR 72 h after the first warfarin dose, then as the dose is adjusted <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>
-   Stop enoxaparin when the INR is in the target range for the valve type, position and risk factors <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>; in range on two consecutive days <span class="tag prop">proposed</span>

| Prosthesis thrombogenicity | No risk factor | Risk factor\* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low (e.g. St Jude, On-X, Carbomedics, Medtronic Hall, ATS) | 2.5 | 3.0 |
| Medium (other bileaflet valves) | 3.0 | 3.5 |
| High (tilting disc such as Björk-Shiley; caged ball such as Starr-Edwards) | 3.5 | 4.0 |

Target INR. \*Risk factors: mitral or tricuspid replacement, previous thromboembolism, AF, mitral stenosis of any degree, LVEF under 35%.

#### Tissue valve, repair, rheumatic AF

-   Tissue mitral valve or mitral repair: warfarin for 3 months <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>
-   Rheumatic AF: warfarin, not rivaroxaban (INVICTUS) <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>
-   Continue secondary penicillin prophylaxis after rheumatic valve surgery

> **Evidence:** ESC/EACTS 2021 valvular heart disease guideline (INR targets by prosthesis and patient risk factors; VKA after bioprosthetic MVR or repair for 3 months). INVICTUS (Connolly et al., NEJM 2022).

### Question

**Q:** A mechanical mitral valve in a patient with AF: INR target (medium-thrombogenicity bileaflet)?
- [x] 3.5
  > ESC/EACTS 2021: medium thrombogenicity 3.0, plus 0.5 with a risk factor such as mitral position or AF.
- [ ] 2.0
- [ ] 2.5
- [ ] 4.5
