## [b4-eso-transhiatal-patho] Pathophysiology and staging: esophageal cancer

**East Africa carries one of the world's highest rates of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma**, along a corridor from Ethiopia and Kenya to Malawi. About nine in ten cases here are **squamous**, and patients are young: in Bomet (Tenwek) 11% were 30 or younger. Risk factors studied in Kenya: **very hot tea**, alcohol (especially home-brewed spirits), tobacco, household smoke, and poor oral health. In high-income countries **adenocarcinoma** of the lower third and junction (from reflux and Barrett's esophagus) predominates.

Chain: Chronic thermal and chemical injury to the squamous mucosa → Dysplasia → Invasive squamous carcinoma → Circumferential growth → **Progressive dysphagia (solids, then liquids), weight loss**

Chain: No serosa: early spread → Lymphatics along the length of the esophagus (neck to coeliac) → **Airway (mid third): tracheo-esophageal fistula**

| T (AJCC 8th) |  |
| --- | --- |
| T1a / T1b | lamina propria or muscularis mucosae / submucosa |
| T2 | muscularis propria |
| T3 | adventitia |
| T4a / T4b | pleura, pericardium, azygos, diaphragm, peritoneum (resectable) / aorta, vertebra, trachea (unresectable) |
| N | by number of nodes: N1 1–2, N2 3–6, N3 7 or more |

Separate clinical, pathological and post-neoadjuvant (yp) stage groups; squamous and adenocarcinoma are grouped differently. **Work-up**: endoscopy and biopsy, CT chest and abdomen, PET-CT for curative candidates, EUS for T and N, **bronchoscopy** for tumors at or above the carina, staging laparoscopy for junctional adenocarcinoma.

**Most patients here present with advanced disease**: palliation of dysphagia (a self-expanding metal stent works fastest; brachytherapy lasts longer) is the commonest intervention.

> **Evidence:** ESCCAPE Kenya/Tanzania/Malawi (Middleton et al., Lancet Glob Health 2022): alcohol accounted for 65% of cases in Kenyan men; very hot drinks OR 3.7 in western Kenya (Int J Cancer 2019). Young patients: Lancet 2002 (Bomet). Staging: Rice et al., Ann Cardiothorac Surg 2017. SIREC (Lancet 2004): stents relieved dysphagia faster, brachytherapy gave more dysphagia-free days (115 vs 82) with fewer complications.

## [th-anat] The esophagus and its neighbors

**Neck**: behind the trachea, the **recurrent laryngeal nerves** in the grooves either side. **Upper chest**: behind the trachea, the **azygos arch** on its right, the aortic arch on its left. **Mid chest**: behind the left main bronchus and the left atrium. **Lower chest**: in front of and to the right of the descending aorta, through the hiatus at T10.

Behind it on the right: the **thoracic duct** between the aorta and the azygos, crossing to the left at T4–T6. The **vagi** form the esophageal plexus on its wall.

## [b4-eso-transhiatal-case] Case: junctional adenocarcinoma in a patient with poor lung function

**Neoadjuvant therapy** first: for adenocarcinoma, perioperative **FLOT** chemotherapy now outperforms CROSS. Then a **transhiatal** esophagectomy (abdomen and neck, no thoracotomy) spares his lungs; the price is a less complete mediastinal lymphadenectomy.

> **Evidence:** ESOPEC (NEJM 2025; 438 patients with adenocarcinoma): perioperative FLOT improved 3-year overall survival over CROSS (57.4% vs 50.7%; HR 0.70). Hulscher et al. (NEJM 2002) and 5-year follow-up (Omloo, Ann Surg 2007): no significant overall survival difference between transhiatal and extended transthoracic resection (34% vs 36%), with fewer pulmonary complications after transhiatal resection.

### Case

A **68-year-old man**, long-standing reflux, dysphagia; endoscopy: an **adenocarcinoma at the gastro-esophageal junction** (Siewert type I–II) on Barrett's mucosa; staging laparoscopy negative; cT3 N1. COPD with **FEV1 45%**.

## [b4-eso-transhiatal-consent] Consent: what to discuss with this patient

**1\. The patient's own risk**: Fitness, nutrition (weight loss, albumin), cardiorespiratory reserve. Quote the figure, not a textbook average.

#### 2\. Common

-   Pneumonia
-   AF
-   Wound infection
-   Hoarse voice (neck anastomosis)

#### 3\. Serious

-   Anastomotic leak
-   Conduit necrosis
-   Chylothorax
-   Recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy
-   Death

#### 4\. Specific to this operation

-   Long-term: reflux (sleep head-up), early satiety, dumping, stricture needing dilatation, weight loss
-   Feeding jejunostomy for weeks

#### 5\. Alternatives

-   Definitive chemoradiation
-   Palliation: stent, radiotherapy
-   No operation, and what that means

#### 6\. Recovery

-   ICU, then ward; drains; walking from day 1
-   Return to normal activity over weeks
-   Follow-up in clinic

#### 7\. Kenya-specific

-   Most present late and malnourished: pre-operative nutrition
-   The realistic chance of cure, and palliation as an option

## [th-lap] Abdomen: mobilize the stomach

Upper midline laparotomy (or laparoscopy). Divide the gastrocolic omentum **well away from the right gastroepiploic arcade**, which the conduit will live on, then the short gastric vessels up to the left crus. Open the lesser omentum. **Kocherise** the duodenum so the pylorus reaches the hiatus; a pyloric drainage procedure or none, by unit policy.

## [th-lga] Left gastric artery at its origin

Lift the stomach up; the **left gastric pedicle** is taut in the lesser sac. Clear the **coeliac nodes** into the specimen and staple or tie the left gastric artery and vein **at their origin**. Check the common hepatic and splenic arteries first.

## [th-hiatus] Transhiatal: blunt dissection from below

Open the hiatus widely (divide the crura forward). Under direct vision, then with the hand flat on the esophagus, free it from the **aorta** behind, the **pericardium** in front and both pleurae, as high as the hand reaches (the carina).

Watch the blood pressure: the heart is compressed by the hand.

### Question

**Q:** During transhiatal blunt dissection the anesthetist reports a large air leak. What has happened?
- [x] A tear in the membranous trachea or left main bronchus
  > The membranous airway lies directly on the esophagus; advance the tube past the tear and repair it through a right thoracotomy or via the neck.
- [ ] A pneumothorax
- [ ] An azygos tear
- [ ] A tear of the left atrium

## [th-neck] Left neck: find the cervical esophagus

Head turned to the right. Incision along the **anterior border of the left sternocleidomastoid**. Retract the **carotid sheath laterally**, divide the omohyoid and the middle thyroid vein, and reach the esophagus on the prevertebral fascia.

The **left recurrent laryngeal nerve** lies in the tracheo-esophageal groove: no metal retractor against it, finger dissection only.

## [th-above] Blunt dissection from above, then divide

From the neck, free the upper esophagus with a finger to meet the hand from below. Divide it in the neck and draw the specimen down into the abdomen.

## [th-conduit] Make the conduit and bring it up

Staple the lesser curvature from below the cardia to make a **4–5 cm tube** of greater curvature on the **right gastroepiploic artery**. Pass it through the posterior mediastinum (the esophageal bed) to the neck in a plastic sleeve, without twisting.

Check the color of the tip: poor perfusion there is what leaks.

## [th-anast] The anastomosis

**Cervical**: hand-sewn single layer or a linear-stapled side-to-side (Orringer). More leaks than in the chest, but a neck leak usually drains through the wound and heals.

Nasogastric tube past the anastomosis; feeding jejunostomy by unit policy.

## [th-after] What goes wrong

**Anastomotic leak** and **conduit necrosis** (fever, arrhythmia, effluent in the drain: contrast study or endoscopy). **Chylothorax** (milky drain output once fed). **Recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy** (hoarseness, aspiration), mostly after neck dissection. Pneumonia above all: early mobilization, physiotherapy, sitting up.

#### Specific to this operation

-   Labs day 1 and day 3: FBC, UEC/LFT, CRP, Ca, Mg, PCT (trend) <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>
-   POD 3 CRP under 17.6 mg/dL (176 mg/L), POD 5 under 13.2 mg/dL (132 mg/L): leak unlikely <span class="tag gl">guideline</span>
-   Jejunostomy feeds from day 1–2 <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>; refeeding precautions in the malnourished (start at no more than 10 kcal/kg/day; PO₄, K⁺, Mg²⁺ daily for 3 days) <span class="tag prop">proposed</span>
-   Oral sips day 6–7 with a clean drain, falling CRP and a well patient <span class="tag knh">KNH practice</span>
-   NG on free drainage, never re-passed blind; head up 30°
-   New AF, fever, rising CRP or PCT, turbid or salivary drain: CT with oral contrast, then endoscopy
